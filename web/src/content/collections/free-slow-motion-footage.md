## Time, stretched

Slow motion shows what the eye misses: a splash hanging in the air, exhaust curling off a rocket engine, a bird's wingbeat. It adds weight to a moment and works as a pause in a fast edit.

## Frame rate is everything

Footage for slow motion is shot at a high frame rate, 50, 60, 120 frames per second or far more for scientific high-speed cameras, and played back at a normal rate. The frame rate in each clip's details tells you how much headroom it has. Some clips here were published already slowed down; others are at their native high frame rate and slow down cleanly in your editor. NASA publishes high-speed footage of launches and tests, and Wikimedia Commons holds slow-motion clips of nature and physics.
