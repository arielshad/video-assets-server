## Two questions, not one

Whether a clip can go into a commercial video depends on two separate things:

1. **The copyright licence.** Does the owner of the footage allow commercial use, and on what conditions (credit, share-alike, no changes)?
2. **What is in the frame.** Do identifiable people, logos, products, artworks or insignia appear, and were releases signed for them?

A clip can pass the first test and fail the second. NASA footage of astronauts is a good example: not copyrighted in the US, but NASA asks for permission before identifiable astronauts appear in ads. That is why every result here carries both a **verdict** and an **editorial** flag.

## The licences, from most to least permissive

| Licence | Commercial use | Conditions | Verdict here |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Public domain, CC0 | Yes | None | Commercial use OK |
| US federal government work | Yes | None in the US; trademarks and people still apply | Commercial use OK |
| NASA media | Yes | Credit NASA, no insignia, no implied endorsement | OK with credit, editorial |
| CC BY | Yes | Credit title, author, source, licence; note changes | OK with credit |
| CC BY-SA | Yes, but | Your whole video must carry the same licence | Restricted |
| CC BY-NC | No | Non-commercial only | Non-commercial only |
| CC BY-ND | In practice no | No adapted versions: trimming and grading inside an edit generally counts | Non-commercial only |
| Free stock site licences (Pexels, Pixabay…) | Yes | No reselling the clips as they are; rules about people | Read on the site |
| Paid stock | After you pay | Per clip or subscription | Needs a paid licence |

## What "commercial" means

Creative Commons defines non-commercial use as use "not primarily intended for or directed towards commercial advantage or monetary compensation". In practice, treat these as commercial: ads and sponsored posts, client and agency work, product and marketing videos, monetised channels, paid courses, and anything made for a business. Personal projects, school assignments and non-profit educational screenings are usually not.

## Where free, commercial-safe footage comes from

- **Wikimedia Commons** only accepts free licences, mostly CC0, public domain, CC BY and CC BY-SA, and records the licence per file.
- **NASA** (the Image and Video Library and the Scientific Visualization Studio) publishes footage that is generally not copyrighted in the US, with credit and endorsement rules.
- **The Internet Archive** holds public-domain and Creative Commons films, but most licences there are set by uploaders, so they need checking.
- **Free stock sites** such as Pexels and Pixabay have their own free licences. They can't be searched automatically, so every search links to the same query there.

Turn on **Commercial-safe** in the search (or `commercial=true` in the API) to keep only the green verdicts, then check the editorial flag before you put a clip in an ad.
